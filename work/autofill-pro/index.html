AutoFill Pro

A Chromium extension for job applications: no accounts, no server, AI features opt-in

Enter your details once, or import them from a resume, then fill an entire application on any site with one click. Everything is stored locally.

44 recognized form fields
46 application platforms
0 servers involved

Overview

Job applications ask the same forty questions in forty different DOM shapes. The tools that solve this usually do it by holding your personal data on their servers, which is a strange trade for a convenience feature.

AutoFill Pro keeps the data in the browser’s local storage and does the matching on the page. There are no accounts and no sign-up: install it, enter your data, and it fills forms with it. Anyone you share it with does the same with theirs.

How it works

  • Field recognition: covers 44 common application fields across 46 tested platforms, matching by structure rather than by per-site rules.
  • Two ways in: type the profile directly, or import a resume PDF or pasted LinkedIn text and review what was extracted before saving.
  • One action to fill: a floating button or Alt+Shift+F. Long-pressing the button previews what will be filled before committing, and an Undo appears after every fill.
  • Chromium-wide: Chrome, Edge, Brave and anything else Chromium-based.

The AI fallback, and its boundary

Some applications ask open-ended questions that no field matcher can answer, such as “why do you want to work here”. AI answers for those are optional and off by default. When enabled, only the question text and non-sensitive profile details are sent.

  • Never transmitted: date of birth, race and ethnicity, disability and veteran status, gender, phone number and salary.
  • Resume import: can also use the AI parser. Your email, phone number and street address are stripped on-device first and merged back locally.
  • With AI off: nothing leaves the device at all.

Privacy posture

No server, no tracking, no accounts. The profile lives in browser local storage and is visible only to the person who typed it. The sensitive-field exclusion list is enforced in the extension code rather than promised in a policy.
