Meeting Bot

No bot joins your call, and the recording never leaves the machine

A meeting notetaker that captures whatever audio is already playing on your Mac, transcribes it locally with Whisper, and ships three front-ends over one engine.

3 front-ends over one core
$0 running cost
0 audio bytes uploaded

Overview

Cloud notetakers solve the problem by uploading your meeting. That is a reasonable engineering choice and an unreasonable privacy one, and it also means the tool only works where a bot can be invited.

Meeting Bot takes the other path. It captures the audio already playing on the Mac — Zoom, Meet, Teams, FaceTime, or a browser tab — so it needs no meeting-platform integration and works everywhere by construction. Transcription runs locally through Whisper. The only optional network call sends finished transcript text for summarization, and that endpoint can be pointed at a local model instead.

How it works

  • System-audio capture through a small Swift helper (audiocap), riding the macOS screen-recording permission that system audio sits behind.
  • Speaker attribution without diarization in the common case: your microphone is “Me” and system audio is “Them”, with optional Speaker A/B/C diarization layered on top.
  • Structured output per meeting — summary, decisions, action items, and the full transcript — rather than an undifferentiated wall of text.
  • Templates for general, standup, 1:1, interview and sales-call shapes, so the summary prompt matches the meeting type.
  • On-device transcript cleanup that strips filler, stutters and false starts, plus on-demand rewrites: key points, formal, short, long.
  • Talk-time analytics per note: Me/Them ratio, word counts, turn counts, longest monologue.
  • Search and ask across every meeting, including Q&A over the archive.

Three front-ends, one engine

The same core is driven by a native macOS SwiftUI app, a Chrome extension, and a CLI, with an iOS companion that records from the microphone on-device. Keeping the engine separate from the surfaces is what makes three front-ends affordable to maintain: each one is a thin driver over the same record, transcribe, summarize and export pipeline.

Results

  • Raw audio and transcripts stay on the machine; the privacy property is enforced by where the compute happens, not by a policy document.
  • Runs at zero cost: local Whisper plus a free inference tier, with a local-model escape hatch for summarization.
  • Exports to Markdown, Apple Notes (iCloud or on-device) and Google Docs.
  • Continuous integration runs on GitHub Actions.
